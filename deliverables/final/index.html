Details

How I worked

  • Tools: Claude Opus 5.5 (in Claude chat and Claude Code) and Poolday. I spent $200 on Claude Max and nothing else.
  • I read the agent guide first. A run takes about an hour, so I started the long Poolday jobs first and did everything else while they ran.
  • Up to 5 Poolday conversations at once: Flam, Wispr Flow, the AI UGC reels, the motion-craft skill and the LinkedIn video, with Claude working next to them.
  • Skills and brand kits first. I made two skills for my org: motion-craft (pacing and motion rules) and ref-teaser (turn a reference video into a teaser). Then a brand kit for each company, from its website.
  • I picked the reference videos myself and wrote what I love in each one, like "the easing, the speed, and the tiles linked by lines".
  • Options before building: actors, then voices, then scripts for the UGC; a direction first for the LinkedIn video.
  • I gave Poolday the goal, not a list of edits. It chose how to do it, as your tip says.
  • Reuse is where the cost drops. A first video for a brand took about 50 minutes (about $140). The same brand again, sent by the loop, took 4 minutes (about $11).

D3 · The agent loop

What it does

  1. Find: it reads the Series B list. A funding-news feed is also built, but it wasn't used in this run.
  2. Quick filter: a score out of 100 in code (how fresh the round is, a named buyer, B2B, video fit). Under 45 is out.
  3. Real scoring: 5 criteria out of 20, each with a written reason. 70 or more is qualified. No named buyer means out.
  4. Poolday: the loop sends the lead to a Poolday automation (a webhook), and Poolday makes the video.
  5. Human check: approve, redo with a note, or reject.
  6. Email: drafted for the named buyer, with a moving video preview. You send it.

The live run (26 Sep)

37 leads → 10 cut by the quick filter → 27 scored → 5 qualified: Flam 99, TwelveLabs 79, Blacksmith 77, Delightree 77 and Wispr Flow 76. Wispr Flow was sent to Poolday. Poolday reused the saved Wispr video and made a new 22-second version in 4 minutes (about $11). I approved it, and the email to Carolyn was drafted.

How I know the scoring makes sense

  • A simple formula with no AI picks 4 of the same 5.
  • It matches the two companies I picked by hand for D2.
  • Every score comes with a written reason you can check.

The real test is the results. Send to the 5, plus a few close calls to compare. Then look at replies and demos by score, and adjust after 20 to 30 sends.

What's still manual, and why

Pasting the video link back into the loop. Poolday's agent can't send it back: it has no internet access, and an automation can only send its result to app connectors, not to a URL. With a "send to webhook" output, that step disappears, and the loop is already built to receive it.

I built the loop with Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code: Python, a local dashboard and 40 tests.

D5 · Growth idea: "Paste a link. Watch the video."

The idea

A free page on poolday.ai. You pick one of three things Poolday already makes well, paste a link, pick a style, leave your work email, and get the video. The main button on the result page is "Book a 15 min demo".

  • Launch video from your website URL. Styles: Apple-like product motion, kinetic type, storytelling film.
  • Podcast clips from an episode. Styles people already search for: Hormozi, Diary of a CEO, MrBeast, Ali Abdaal.
  • AI product ad from a URL and a product photo. Styles: 3D hero, giant product in a real city, UGC, cinematic.

It's the Submagic trick (upload, pick "Hormozi", done), applied to Poolday's best outputs.

Why it books demos

  • People see their own brand in a Poolday video before any call.
  • Only work emails get a video. So every video is also a lead with a name, a role and a company.
  • The result page asks for the call at the best moment: "Want this for your real launch? It takes one call."

How people find it

  • "Poolday can actually do it": on X, many posts claim "AI made this video with one prompt", and often it's fake. We remake the video for real in Poolday and reply with the run and the link.
  • The "AI motion designer" trend: right now on X, people post "I paid $1,000 for a video like this a year ago, now I made it with Opus 5.5 in 30 minutes". We post before/after remakes of well-known brands' videos, 2–3 a week, made in Poolday, with the prompt. My D6 remakes show the format.

Budget

$15k in 3 stages: a $2k manual pilot for 2 weeks, then a $5k public page, then $8k to scale. Each stage opens only if the one before hits its target.

At about $19 per video and 5% of videos turning into a demo, that's about $380 per booked demo (estimate). The pilot's job is to push both numbers down. A repeat video on a saved kit already cost about $11 in D3.

Risks

  • A bad video of someone's own product is worse than none. Only ship styles that pass a 10-input test, and have a person check each video.
  • AI ads are the expensive product. Give them only to qualified leads, with a daily credit cap.
  • Motion already does "URL → launch video". Lead with podcast clips and ads, show quality in public, and offer the human call.

D6 · Video review

Upflow (23s): a great idea, but slow

  • The hook takes about 10 seconds.
  • The question at the start ("Did Acme pay invoice #2041 yet?") is never answered, because the product never shows up.
  • The best line (WorkMotion cut invoices 31+ days overdue by 79%) is tiny text in the footer.
  • At 1.5× speed it already feels better. The ideas are good; the shots just last too long.

Top 3 changes: show the product answering the question · cut every shot to reading time + 0.3s (14–16s total) · give −79% its own full card.

PostHog (24s): beautiful, but not PostHog

  • It uses 3D clay renders, but PostHog is a 2D brand (flat, hand-drawn hedgehogs).
  • The big moment ("It's live") is the smallest text in the film.
  • The product only gets 2 seconds of tiny tabs.
  • The end card looks like a web footer, and it uses the old logo.

Top 3 changes: go 2D, in PostHog's own style · an animated sign-off instead of the slide · real product shots at full size.

The big finding: Poolday's videos are too slow

I see it in all three videos I looked at: these two, and a video picked at random from your LinkedIn, where the first 3 seconds are just a blur clearing. People decide to scroll in about 3 seconds.

  • Something real in the first frame: the product, a claim, a face or a number.
  • No blur-in or fade-in at the start.
  • Shots around 1.2 seconds on average.
  • Text stays on screen for reading time plus 0.3 seconds.
  • The hook is said by 2 seconds.

The fix for the product: a "social pacing" setting, on by default for social formats. And the brand kit should learn the brand's style of visuals (PostHog = 2D drawings), not only its logo, colours and fonts.

About the remakes

I built them in code with Claude Opus 5.5, not in Poolday, to show exactly what I'd ask for. Upflow: 23 → 16 seconds, and the product shows up at 6 seconds. PostHog: 24 → 20 seconds, in 2D with PostHog's real hedgehogs and the new logo. The PostHog one is private: the hedgehogs are their licensed art.

Feedback on Poolday, from using it

  • Great: the AI UGC (the creator filming the demo), the questions in Align mode, and honest answers from the agent.
  • Pacing: add a default "social pacing" setting (see D6).
  • Automations can be started from outside but can't report back. Add a "send to webhook" output with the video link.
  • API: the agent offers "Create an API key", but the page says integrations aren't enabled. It should offer "Request access" instead.
  • Small bug: the "Upload your assets" window cuts off its text on the right.
