# D1: LinkedIn post + video (prep)

**Where it's posted:** the Poolday hiring manager's personal account (founder or exec voice).
**Goal:** get people excited and bring in **many qualified leads**: B2B marketers, founders and heads of growth at startups.
**Video:** made in Poolday. 4:5, under 45s, works with the sound off.

> Fill-in rule: every `[bracket]` gets a **real measured number** from `METHODS.md` (prompts, time, credits). No invented customers, stats or Poolday claims. If a number isn't flattering, reword the line. Don't fake it.

---

## 1. Three use-case angles, ranked

### #1 (recommended): "One URL in, brand kit + launch video out"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Use case** | Give Poolday only a company URL. It pulls the logo, colors, fonts and product screenshots into a brand kit (screenshots get rebuilt into animatable pieces), pitches concept directions, then builds a launch video. |
| **Why the leads are qualified** | The CTA asks for a **company URL**, and people only paste a URL for a company they care about. Every comment arrives pre-enriched (domain → size, funding, B2B or not). The pain it solves ("we have no launch video, and an agency takes weeks") belongs to marketing and growth leads at startups. It is also the exact flow of the growth idea (D5), so the post doubles as a live test of that funnel. |
| **Hook (0–2s)** | A URL typed into a bar, Enter, then a **hard cut to a frame of the finished video**. On-screen text: **"This URL → this video."** The input-to-output story is readable in 2 seconds with no audio. |
| **3-beat arc** | **1. Input (2–8s):** the URL pasted into Poolday, real screen footage, sped up. **2. Build (8–22s):** the brand kit assembles: logo, palette and fonts snap in, screenshots break apart into layers, then the 5 concept directions appear as a grid and one gets picked. **3. Payoff (22–40s):** 12–15s of the finished launch video at full size, then an end card: **"Comment your URL. We'll make yours."** |
| **Format** | 4:5, 35–45s, captions and on-screen labels burned in, no voice-over needed. Music is optional. |
| **Poolday inputs** | The URL (Poolday's own URL by default, see Risk), Align mode, Max tier, the brief in section 4, and a screen recording of the run (captured by us, since Poolday doesn't record itself). |
| **Risk** | *Whose URL?* Using another company's brand publicly needs their OK, so **default to poolday.ai** (safe, and it shows Poolday on itself) or a company that agrees in writing. *Honesty:* a run takes about an hour, so never imply "in 60 seconds". Say "[N] prompts, [T] hours, [M] minutes of my time". *Weak output* on an arbitrary site: run 2–3 URLs and show the best one. |

### #2: "I made a launch video for a company that just raised. Here's one."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Use case** | Personalized prospect videos (D2) for fresh Series B companies, made in Poolday and turned into a reusable `/prospect-video` command, so the second company takes about 2 prompts. Ties to the agent loop (D3): find the round → brand kit → video → human check → email. |
| **Why the leads are qualified** | It speaks to funded startups at their exact buying moment (new round → launch and hiring push). It also pulls in heads of growth and outbound people who want the *system*, and they're buyers too. The CTA can filter on "raised in the last 6 months" or "launching in the next 90 days". |
| **Hook (0–2s)** | A funding headline card, **"$[X]M Series B · [N] days ago"**, that cracks open into the first frame of their personalized video. Text: **"They raised. I made their launch video."** |
| **3-beat arc** | **1. Trigger (2–8s):** the round as a news card, then their homepage. **2. Build (8–20s):** brand kit + reference video side by side, the single review step, "Saved as /prospect-video". **3. Payoff (20–40s):** the teaser, then a fast cut showing video #2 for another company ("2 prompts"), then the end card. |
| **Format** | 4:5, under 45s, burned-in captions. The prospect teaser is 16:9, so letterbox it inside the 4:5 frame with labels above and below. |
| **Poolday inputs** | The finished D2 videos, a screen recording of the `/prospect-video` call, and Align mode for the wrapper edit. |
| **Risk** | **Showing a real company's brand without consent.** Only post the video of a company that has replied positively and OK'd it (so this angle depends on D2 email timing). Otherwise it can look like using their raise for our marketing. It is also less universal than #1: people who haven't raised may scroll past. |

### #3: "We remade a viral 'AI made this in one prompt' video, for real"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Use case** | Take a viral "one prompt made this" motion video (often faked), let Poolday pull it from the link, remake it, and show the real process. Ties to the growth idea's X reply play. |
| **Why the leads are qualified** | **Mostly they aren't.** It reaches AI enthusiasts, motion designers and creators. There's lots of excitement but few B2B buyers. It's good for reach and weak for demos. |
| **Hook (0–2s)** | A split screen: the viral post's claim ("one prompt") next to "Actually: [N] prompts, [T]". |
| **3-beat arc** | **1.** The claim. **2.** The real remake running in Poolday (reference pulled from the link, concept options). **3.** Side-by-side result, then "what it actually takes". |
| **Format** | 4:5, under 45s, burned-in captions. |
| **Poolday inputs** | The viral video link (YouTube, TikTok or IG; Poolday can't read Drive or Dropbox links) and a brief about what to keep. |
| **Risk** | IP and "dunking on a creator" optics coming from an exec account, and low lead quality. **Better used on X** (D5 distribution) than as the hiring manager's LinkedIn post. |

**Ranking logic:** #1 has the widest qualified audience, needs no third-party consent, and tests the growth-idea funnel. #2 is the strongest proof of the system, but depends on prospect consent. #3 gets reach without qualified leads.

## 2. Post copy (top 2 angles, 2 variants each)

LinkedIn rules applied: the first 2 lines work as a standalone hook before "see more", short lines, one concrete proof point, a CTA that asks for a URL (the qualifier), and the link in the first comment (external links in the body cut reach).

```
I gave our video agent one thing: a URL.
It came back with a brand kit and a launch video.

No logo files. No screenshots. No brief doc.

What it did on its own:
→ read the site and pulled logo, colors, fonts
→ rebuilt the product screenshots into pieces it could animate
→ pitched 5 concept directions (I picked one)
→ built the video

[N] prompts. About [T] hours of run time. [M] minutes of mine.

Most startups ship without a launch video because it means an agency
and two weeks. That's the problem we're working on at Poolday.

Want one for your company?
Comment VIDEO + your website.
I'll pick [10] B2B startups and make theirs this week.

(Link to try it yourself is in the first comment.)
```

### Angle #1, Variant B: "the opinion"

```
A launch video shouldn't need a kickoff call.
So I tested it: one URL in, finished video out.

The video below started as a single link.
Poolday built the brand kit from the site, showed me 5 directions,
I chose one, and it made the rest.

My total input: [N] prompts and one decision.

This is what I want every B2B team to have:
```

**First comment (both):**
`Try it on your own site: [poolday.ai link with UTM, e.g. ?utm_source=linkedin&utm_campaign=d1-url-video]. The video above: [N] prompts, [T] hours of run time, nothing edited outside Poolday.`

### Angle #2, Variant A: "the case"

```
[Company] raised a $[X]M Series B [N] days ago.
Here's the launch video I made for them. (Shared with their OK.)

Why them: a fresh round means a launch and hiring push.
That's exactly when a team needs video, and has the least time for it.

How:
→ brand kit built from their website
→ a reference video I liked, for pacing
→ one review step before the build
→ then I saved the flow as a command

Company #2 took [2] prompts.

If you raised in the last 6 months and have a launch coming,
comment VIDEO + your URL. I'll make yours.

Link in the first comment.
```

### Angle #2, Variant B: "the system"

```
Every week, B2B startups announce a round.
Almost all of them are about to launch something.

So I built a small loop:
find the round → build their brand kit → make a personalized video
→ a human checks it → a short email.

The video below is one output, for [Company] (posted with their OK).
The first one took [7] prompts. Now it takes [2].

Personal video beats a cold email with a PDF.
And it's finally cheap enough to do for every account.

Heads of growth: comment VIDEO + your URL
and I'll run your company through the same loop.

Link in the first comment.
```

**First comment (both):** `How the loop works + make your own: [link with UTM]. Credits used for this video: [$].`

**Voice checklist:** first person, plain verbs, and numbers instead of adjectives. No "revolutionary", "game-changer" or "excited to announce". No emoji. Arrows are the only symbols.

## 3. Comment → lead handling (runs through the D3 agent loop)

**Promise capacity up front** ("I'll pick [10]"). It creates scarcity, caps credit spend, and makes the selection itself a reason to qualify.

### Flow
```
Comment "VIDEO + URL"
 → [0–1h] Reply to every comment publicly ("On it" / "Got it, reviewing today").
          Replies in the first hour drive reach.
 → [every 2–3h] Export the comments (copy manually or use a comment-export tool)
          into the loop's lead CSV: name, headline, URL, comment text.
 → Enrich: website → B2B?, product with UI?, employees, last round + date,
          commenter's role.
 → Score (ICP rubric 0–100) → tier A / B / C  (below)
 → Tier A: loop generates the "/prospect-video <url>" call → human runs it in Poolday
          → HUMAN GATE (is it good enough to post publicly with their name on it?)
          → reply to their comment with the video (public proof = more reach)
          + DM: "Made this for [Company]. Want the editable version? 15 min: [demo link]"
 → Tier B: lighter version (brand kit + short teaser, Light tier), batched,
          delivered by DM with the demo link.
 → Tier C: friendly reply + link to try it themselves. No credits spent.
 → Log everything: lead, tier, video link, credits, reply, demo booked (yes/no).
```

### Prioritizing commenters (ICP rubric)
| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Role: founder, CEO, CMO, head or VP of marketing, growth, product marketing, brand | +30 |
| B2B company with a product UI (SaaS, dev tools, fintech, AI tools) | +25 |
| Raised in the last 6 months, or states "launching X" in the comment | +20 |
| 10–500 employees (has a marketing budget, no in-house motion team) | +15 |
| Mentioned what they're launching (the Variant B CTA asks for it) | +10 |
| **Disqualify or route elsewhere:** agencies and freelancers (→ partner list), students, competitors, personal sites, B2C apps with no marketing team | 0 / tag |

**Tier A ≥ 70** (the [10] promised videos, strongest first). **Tier B 40–69.** **Tier C < 40.**
Tie-break: bigger reach first (their public repost of the video is free distribution), then earliest comment.

### Guardrails
- Nothing gets posted in someone's thread without the human gate. A weak video in public hurts more than no video.
- The deadline is written in the post ("this week"). Deliver within 72h or say so publicly.
- Credit cap: set a D1 budget (e.g. [$300] of the $2,000) and track it per video in `METHODS.md`.

## 4. Poolday brief for the top angle (intent only, 107 words)

```
Objective: a LinkedIn video for our founder's post proving that one company URL
is enough for you to build a brand kit and a launch video. Success = B2B viewers
comment their URL to get one.
Audience: founders and marketing/growth leads at B2B startups, on mobile, sound off.
Format: 4:5, under 45s, captions burned in, fully readable without audio.
Hook: the first 2 seconds must show input → output (URL → finished frame).
Validation: give me 5 concept directions first; I pick before you build.
Fixed: real footage of this run, nothing faked; end card "Comment your URL".
Creative freedom: style, pacing, music, transitions, how you show the process.
```
Input alongside it: the URL (`poolday.ai` by default) plus our screen recording of the brand-kit run. Settings: Align mode, Max tier.

## 5. Success metrics

| Metric | Why | Proposed target (vs the account's own baseline) |
|---|---|---|
| **Comments containing a URL** | The primary lead count | ≥ 40 in 72h |
| **% of URL comments scoring Tier A or B** | Lead *quality*: proves the CTA qualifies | ≥ 50% |
| **Demos booked from post leads** (DM link + first-comment UTM) | The actual goal | ≥ [8–10] within 14 days |
| Videos delivered / promised | Keeps the promise | 100% of Tier A within 72h |
| Cost per qualified lead | Credits spent ÷ Tier A+B leads | Track, for the D5 budget case |
| Impressions and engagement rate | Excitement and reach | ≥ 2× the founder's median post |
| Reposts of the delivered videos by recipients | Second-wave distribution | Track (each one is free reach) |
| First-comment link clicks (UTM) → signups | Self-serve intent | Track |
| Sound-off check (pre-launch) | The hook works muted | 3 of 3 colleagues explain the video after 3s muted |

Read the numbers at 24h (reach), 72h (leads + delivery) and 14 days (demos).
