# D1: LinkedIn post + video

**Posted from:** the Poolday hiring manager's personal account (founder voice). **Goal:** reach, and many *qualified* leads (B2B founders, marketers, growth leads). **Video:** made in Poolday, 4:5, ≤45s, readable on mute.
**Fill-in rule:** every `[bracket]` gets a real measured number from `METHODS.md`. No invented stats, customers or Poolday claims.

**The moment (checked 2026-09-24, sources at the bottom):** Opus 5.5 shipped on Tue 22 Sep [S1] and GPT-6 Astra on 3 Sep [S2]. Both set off a wave of "made this video with one prompt" posts, whose own fine print says hours of agent work or a second pass [S3][S4]. At the same time, "AI slop" is the backlash word of the year. LinkedIn added a "Seems like AI slop" button on 30 Jul that cuts the reach of flagged posts, and people clicked it over 1M times in its first month [S5][S6]. Sora's app shut down in April, and its API is removed today [S7]. Brands like McDonald's and Valentino pulled AI ads after backlash [S8]. Motion now ships "drop a product URL, get a launch video" inside Claude [S9], so our old "URL in → video out" angle is no longer new on its own.

## 1. Angles, ranked (V = virality, L = lead quality, F = feasibility in 48h, each out of 5)

**#1 The AI Video Slop Test (V5 · L4 · F3).** One B2B launch brief goes through the arena leaders (Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0 [S10]), a "one prompt" Claude/Remotion setup [S11] and Poolday, with the results side by side.
- *Why now:* it rides the slop backlash and the model launches together, and "same prompt, every model" comparisons already get shared [S10]. Marketers asking "will AI video embarrass my brand?" are buyers.
- *Video:* **0–2s** a 2×3 grid of all outputs playing at once, text "Same brief. 6 AI video tools. Spot the slop." **Beat 1 (2–8s):** the brief on screen. **Beat 2 (8–30s):** each clip with its slop tell circled (melted logo, made-up UI, gibberish text) plus its cost line (prompts, minutes). **Beat 3 (30–45s):** Poolday's clip full frame, the scorecard, end card "Comment TEST + your URL". 4:5, 40–45s.
- *Production:* brand kit of the test product (a consenting D2 company, else poolday.ai) and one Poolday run of the *identical* brief (≈7 prompts on a first video). The raw model clips are made outside Poolday: small extra spend, check each tool's pricing. Then one Poolday edit run for the comparison video (brief in §3). About 5–7h of work.
- *CTA:* "Comment TEST + your company URL. I'll run [10] B2B startups through it this week."
- *Main risk:* fairness, and Poolday not clearly winning. Fix the rules before starting (same brief, same screenshot, ≤60 min of my time per tool, score criteria published in the first comment) and publish whatever comes out. If a model wins on some criterion, say so. That honesty is what makes it credible.

**#2 "One prompt" receipts: remake this week's viral Opus 5.5 video in Poolday (V5 · L3 · F4).**
- *Why now:* Opus 5.5 is two days old, and one-prompt motion videos are all over X and Threads [S3][S4]. Pieces that unpack what "one prompt" really took are already circulating [S4], so the honest-receipts angle has an audience.
- *Video:* **0–2s** split screen: the original on the left, "one prompt"; ours on the right, "[N] prompts · [T]". **Beat 1:** the post link pasted into Poolday as the reference. **Beat 2:** the "show me 3 directions first" step, then the pick. **Beat 3:** both playing in sync, then the receipts card. 4:5, ≤40s.
- *Production:* the link (YouTube/TikTok/IG only, no Drive links), 1 run of ≈5–7 prompts, our screen recording. About 3h.
- *CTA:* "Comment REMAKE + your URL + a style you like."
- *Main risk:* looking like a dunk on a creator from an exec account, plus IP. Pick a launch-style (B2B-looking) piece, get the creator's OK, credit them, and don't mock the claim.

**#3 Slop teardown: "5 tells your launch video was made by AI" (V4 · L4 · F5).** Best as the follow-up post 3–4 days after #1, reusing its footage.
- *Why now:* LinkedIn's slop button and the brand ad backlash [S5][S8] make marketers afraid of their own AI output.
- *Video:* **0–2s** a slop frame with a red circle, "Tell #1". **Beats:** 5 tells (melting text, fake UI, off-brand colors, morphing hands, stock-AI look), each fixed by the brand-kit version. 4:5, 30s.
- *Production:* no new runs, only 1 edit run in Poolday. About 2h.
- *CTA:* "Comment SLOP + your URL and I'll check your homepage video for the 5 tells."
- *Main risk:* reads as a negative listicle. It needs the fixes on screen, not only the tells.

**#4 One URL in, launch video out (V2 · L5 · F5).** The old #1. It is the safest and most qualified, but Motion now ships the same promise [S9]. Keep it as the **CTA mechanic** of #1–#3 (people comment their URL and get a video), not as the hook.

**Ranking logic:** virality decides whether any leads come at all, so #1 and #2 lead. #1 wins because its audience is people choosing video tools (buyers), while #2 draws AI enthusiasts. #1 also sets up #3 for free.

## 3. Poolday brief for #1 (the comparison video, 108 words)
```
Context: I ran one identical brief through [6] AI video tools; their clips and
your own output for that brief are attached, labelled, with a scorecard.
Objective: a LinkedIn video for our founder's post "The AI Video Slop Test".
Success = B2B marketers comment their company URL.
Audience: founders and marketing leads, on mobile, sound off.
Output: 4:5, 40–45s, captions burned in.
Hard constraints: every clip shown as generated, same label style for every tool,
no claim beyond the scorecard.
Validation: first show me 3 hook frames and 2 grid layouts; I pick, then you build.
Creative freedom: pacing, transitions, music, how the slop tells are called out.
```
**The test brief** (the same text for every tool, Poolday included, Build mode): *"15-second launch video for [product, URL]. Use this logo and this product screenshot. Line: '[one claim]'. 4:5, readable without sound. End on logo + URL."*

## 4. Comment → lead (plugs into `loop/`)
1. **Reply to every comment within 1h** ("On it"). Early replies drive reach.
2. **Every 3h, export the comments** into `data/linkedin-comments.csv` with the loop's columns: `Company, Domain, Contact 1, Contact 1 title, Contact 1 LinkedIn URL, What they do` (the comment text goes in "What they do"). Then run `LEADS_CSV=../data/linkedin-comments.csv make seed serve`. Commenters have no round date, so freshness scores 0 and the pre-score tops out at 60. Either set `PRESCORE_GATE=30` or add the last round from enrichment.
3. **Score ≥70 (Tier A, the [10] promised):** the `/prospect-video` prompt → Poolday → **human gate** → reply in their thread with the video and a DM with the demo link. **40–69:** batch a lighter version, sent by DM. **<40, agencies, students:** a friendly reply and the self-serve link, with no credits spent.
4. **Guardrails:** nothing public without the human gate. Deliver within 72h or say so publicly. D1 credit cap [$300] of $2,000, tracked per video in `METHODS.md`.
