# D4: AI UGC prep (Instagram Reels)

**Goal:** 4–19 AI UGC Reels, all made in Poolday, that drive B2B marketers, founders, growth people and social/content managers (startups and agencies) to Poolday.
**Constraint:** new account, so ~1–2 posts/day. That means about 5 posts inside the 48h window, with the rest scheduled after.
**Budget:** ~$500 of credits for UGC (RUNBOOK credit table).

**Rules applied to every script**
- 9:16, 15–30s, burned-in captions, hook on screen in the first frame (readable with sound off).
- Keep text out of the bottom ~20% and the right edge, where the IG UI sits.
- Product claims only use what the agent guide documents: brand kit from a URL, a screenshot rebuilt into animatable pieces, Align mode (one question at a time), picking actors and voices before building, variants in one conversation, translation, the live editor with point & select, and runs that take about an hour. **Before posting, check every claim against what you actually saw in your own run.**
- Characters are **personas in POV/skit framing**, never "real customers". See section 6.

## 1. Four angles

| # | Angle | Format | Why it hooks this audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **POV pain** | "POV: your CEO wants a launch video by Friday." A character reacts to a deadline, then shows the fix. | Instant self-recognition for marketers and social managers. POV framing is native to Reels and is clearly a skit, which keeps compliance simple. |
| **B** | **"This is AI" reveal** | Before/after reveal. The actor reveals that they, the voice and the set are AI, then shows how the video was made (actor grid, voice picker, render). | Pattern interrupt plus curiosity ("wait, really?"). This is the product demo itself: the video is the proof. AI disclosure becomes the hook instead of a liability. Drives comments ("no way that's AI"). |
| **C** | **Confession / "stopped paying for"** | A founder or agency persona lists what they no longer outsource, and what they still pay humans for. | List format holds retention (viewers wait for #3). The "still pay humans for taste" line makes it believable and not anti-agency ad-speak. Hits the budget pain for founders. |
| **D** | **Steal my workflow** | Screen-heavy tutorial: 3 steps, one pro tip ("say 'hard cut at 0:04', not 'punchier'"). | Earns saves and shares, which is the signal that carries a new account. It attracts the operators who actually buy tools. It is low-risk because it teaches real, documented behavior. |

*"I tested X AI video tools":* only make this if you actually test them. Show real outputs, and don't name or knock competitors with claims you can't back. Otherwise fold it into D ("what I look for in an AI video tool").

## 4. Posting plan

### First 5, ranked
| Rank | Script | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **S2 "Nothing here is real"** | Strongest pattern interrupt, and the disclosure is the hook, so it's the safest opener for a new account. "Comment VIDEO" builds early engagement signals. It also shows the Poolday actor/voice flow. |
| 2 | **S1 "Friday deadline"** | The most relatable pain for the core ICP (marketers). Shows the brand kit → video flow end to end. |
| 3 | **S5 "3 steps"** | Save/share bait. Tests whether educational content beats skits for this audience. |
| 4 | **S3 "Stopped paying an agency"** | Founder angle and list retention. Tests the budget-pain message. |
| 5 | **S8 "Make it punchier"** | Contrarian, and pulls operators. A second comment-CTA data point to compare with S2. |

The first 5 cover all 4 angles and mix CTAs (2× comment, 3× link in bio), so after 48h you know which angle and which CTA to double down on.
**Queue after 48h:** S6, S7, S4, then hook-swapped versions of the winners (up to 19 total).

### Schedule
Audience is mostly US startups, so post in **ET on weekdays**, around **12:00–13:00** (lunch scroll) and **18:00–20:00** (evening). These are starting points: switch to the account's own insights once it has any data.

| Slot | Post |
|---|---|
| Day 1, 12:30 ET | S2 |
| Day 1, 19:00 ET | S1 |
| Day 2, 12:30 ET | S5 |
| Day 2, 19:00 ET | S3 |
| Day 3, 12:30 ET (or last slot inside 48h) | S8 |
| Then 1/day | S6 → S7 → S4 → winner variants |

If the account seems throttled (views well below the first post, or an action block), drop to 1/day.

**"Comment VIDEO":** reply by DM with the brief or template plus the Poolday link, either manually (fine at this volume) or with a DM auto-reply tool. Each DM counts as a lead touch, so log it.
**Bio link:** Poolday URL with UTM (`?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=ugc&utm_campaign=d4&utm_content=<post-id>`). Change `utm_content` when a new post is pinned or goes live, or use a link-in-bio page with one tagged link per post.

### What to measure (log in a sheet per post)
| Metric | When | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Views | +1h, +24h, +48h | +1h = hook/initial test result. +24h = the number you report. |
| Avg watch time / % watched, and skip rate if shown | +24h | Did the hook (0–2s) and the body hold? |
| Shares, saves | +24h | Distribution signal. Best predictor of reach for the next post. |
| Comments (and "VIDEO" comments) | +24h | Engagement plus leads captured |
| Profile visits → external link taps | +24h | The funnel to Poolday |
| UTM sessions / signups / demo requests | +48h | The actual leads (ask Poolday or the hiring manager for the analytics view if you can't see it) |

### How to iterate
1. At +24h, rank posts by **views × % watched**, then by link taps and "VIDEO" comments.
2. **Winning hook:** in the same Poolday conversation, produce **3 variants of that script with new hooks from the same angle** (same body, cloned base, cheap). Post one per day.
3. **Winning angle:** write the next 2 scripts in that angle.
4. **Low-view posts with good % watched:** the hook failed and the body works. Swap the hook only.
5. **High views, no link taps:** the CTA is the problem. Switch "link in bio" to "comment VIDEO" (or the reverse).
6. Leave posts that underperform up (mass-deleting on a new account looks odd). Just don't repeat what they did.

## 5. Poolday production flow (intent, not a mega-prompt)

One conversation, **Align mode**. Use **Light** for the photo/voice selection steps and your normal tier for the final renders. Let Poolday decide the editing.

1. **Brand kit (a separate conversation, reusable):** "Create a brand kit for Poolday from poolday.ai." You'll use it for any branded end card or screen inserts.
2. **Brief (short):** "I'm making 8 Instagram Reels (9:16, 15–30s) of AI UGC characters talking to camera for B2B marketers, founders and agency social leads. Casual, phone-shot, believable, not ad-like. Burned-in captions, hook text on screen from frame 1, text kept out of IG's UI zones. I'll give you the scripts and screen inserts."
3. **Validation step 1:** "First generate 30 actor photos across three personas (startup marketing lead, founder, agency social lead) in realistic home and office settings. Let me choose 3."
4. **Validation step 2:** "Now 5 voices for each chosen actor. Let me choose."
5. **Pilot:** "Build S2 only." Send mechanism-level feedback ("cut to the actor grid at 0:03", "caption font larger, move it up above the bottom 20%"). Budget ~5–7 prompts for this first one, per the guide.
6. **Lock the style:** "Save this as a skill and a `/ugc-reel` command that takes a script + actor + voice."
7. **Batch:** "Now produce S1, S3, S5, S8 as variants in this conversation, reusing the base." After that, the rest. Hook swaps later = "same as S2, new hook: H6".
8. **Polish in the live editor:** ask for controls ("headline text, caption size, music volume") and fix typos without re-rendering.

Screen inserts: upload your own screen recordings of real Poolday runs, as files and not Drive links. Log the prompts, time and credits of each step in METHODS.md.

## 6. Compliance

- **Label as AI.** Toggle Instagram's **"AI info" label** when posting every Reel. It is required for realistic AI-generated people/voices under Meta's policy. Also add "AI-generated with Poolday" in each caption and in the bio (for example, "AI characters. Every video made with Poolday.").
- **Personas, not testimonials.** Characters speak in POV/skit framing ("POV: founder…"). They never claim to be real Poolday customers, never give invented metrics ("we 3x'd signups"), and never name real companies as their employer or client.
- **No fake proof.** Slack/email/client messages on screen are clearly fictional, with generic names and no real brands or logos.
- **No real likenesses.** Don't prompt for, or approve, actors resembling real people or creators.
- **Relationship disclosure.** The account promotes Poolday on Poolday's behalf, so the bio should say so (for example, "Made by/for Poolday"). Use Instagram's paid-partnership label if the hiring manager wants it on the brand side.
- **True product claims only.** Every capability shown must be something the product actually did in your run (see the rules at the top).
