Assignment item 5 · Growth idea

Pick a product, paste a link, get the video.

A free page built on three things Poolday already does well: a launch video from a website URL, podcast clips from an episode link, and an AI product ad from a URL and a product photo. One input, one style card, and the video arrives. It is the Submagic mechanic (upload, pick "Hormozi", done) applied to Poolday's best outputs. Every video ends on a booking offer, and credits only go to work emails, so every video is also a qualified lead.

How it works

  1. Pick a product and paste the link.
  2. Upload a screen recording, or let Poolday AI record a walkthrough.
  3. Upload a brand kit, or let Poolday AI build one from the site.
  4. Pick a style: Apple Motion Style, Kinetic Typo or Storytelling Film (podcasts: Hormozi, Diary of a CEO, MrBeast, Ali Abdaal, Iman Gadzhi).
  5. Work email, role, how they heard about Poolday. "Last step. Your video is already being made."
  6. The video plays on the page or arrives by email, depending on render time.

How it spreads

Viral posts claim "[tool] made this video with one prompt". We feed the clip to Poolday as a reference, screen-record the run, and reply from Poolday's account with the result and the link: "Poolday can actually do it. Full run, no hand edits." One reply per thread, only when the remake is at least as good, never accusing anyone.

Why it beats Motion

Motion announced "product URL to launch video" on 2 August [verify]. Our answer is three products instead of one (podcast clips bring people back weekly), generative ads, and a human call behind every qualified video.

Cost and budget

Poolday's site quotes ~$5–$25 per finished video. A bottom-up estimate from public model prices lands in the same range. The real figure comes from the credits used in the first runs.

Real risks

  • Uneven quality on random inputs. Only styles that pass a 10-input test ship, and a person checks each video before it goes out.
  • AI ad cost. Ads go to qualified leads only, with a daily credit cap per product.
  • Motion owns "URL to launch video". Lead with podcast clips and ads, and prove quality publicly with the remakes.
Assignment item 6 · Video review

Upflow and PostHog: strong ideas, slowed down by pacing and brand fit.

Both videos were read frame by frame (one frame per second plus cut detection), then written up as timed fixes.

Upflow, "faster payments" (23s)

The idea is excellent: one question typed into search ("Did Acme pay invoice #2041 yet?") multiplies into dozens of questions in five languages. Three things hold it back.

  • The product never appears. The question is never answered. The missing shot: the Upflow UI showing invoice #2041 → Paid, status turning green.
  • Holds run long. Four seconds to type one sentence, four more to type a six-letter logo. Every shot should last its reading time plus a beat, for about 15 seconds in total.
  • The best proof is micro-text. "WorkMotion cut invoices 31+ days overdue by 79%" deserves its own card.

PostHog, GenAI launch (24s)

High production value and a clear arc (waiting, ship it, it's live, too many tools, one place). Three fixes:

  • Off-brand medium. PostHog is flat 2D illustration and hand-drawn hedgehogs. The 3D clay renders read as generic AI.
  • The end card is a slide. A static logo over a web footer (six icons, a paragraph, a pill) for 3.5 seconds. It should be an animated sign-off: logo, one line, URL.
  • The product shows for two seconds, in tiny tabs. One chart drawing itself at full frame would do more.
What I'd change in the agent, not just the video. Both issues repeat, so fix them once. A saved "SaaS UI pacing" skill (hook 1.5–2.5s, average shot ≤1.2s, text held for reading time plus 0.3s, a logo lockup of 2–3s that keeps moving, always answer the opening question with the real product), plus a brand kit that captures the brand's medium (PostHog: "2D, flat, illustrated, no 3D"), not only its colours and logo.
Assignment item 7 · Page reviews

Home, B2B startups and pricing, rebuilt for more demos.

Each "before" is the live page. Each "after" keeps the live structure and changes only the numbered items. Every change comes with an A/B test measured on completed demo bookings per visitor.

Pricing

1

A framed credits box in each card, as Submagic does: "$1,250 in credits · ≈ 50–250 finished videos". It answers "what do I get?" where the price is read.

2

"Your first month at $600 · no lock-in": the lowest-risk first step, stated at the price.

3–5

Customer logos under the plans, a short comparison against agency, freelancer and in-house editor, and four FAQs built only from facts on the live site.

Home

1

"Watch a 2-min build" next to the demo button, for visitors not ready to call.

2

The video feed grouped into use-case rows (launch films, demos and explainers, ads and testimonials, podcasts) so a visitor finds their case in one glance.

3–4

"Meet Poolday." gets its definition, "An agent that plans, makes and fixes the whole video.", and a "Book a 15 min demo" button right after the four features.

5–6

Customers separated from investors in the logo wall, and the closing card says what happens in the 15 minutes.

B2B startups

1

H1 "Every feature you ship, on video.": the startup's job, not the product category.

2

"View examples" becomes "Paste your URL": a free launch video made from the site, marked with a small "NEW" sticker. It is the growth idea above, placed where intent is highest.

3

Real customer logos in the first screen: PostHog, Lovable, ClickUp, Dust, Marblism, FullEnrich.

4

The nine use cases get animated previews, input turning into output, like the "Meet Poolday" features.

What I kept

The restraint (black ground, off-white buttons, the halftone), the recipe captions that teach the inputs, the "Tech startups use Poolday for" grid with "See the prompt", the "Why tech startups love Poolday" cards, and "Book a 15 min demo" as the one main action.

To confirm before shipping: whether the demo call includes a live brand-kit build, and permission to show each customer logo.
