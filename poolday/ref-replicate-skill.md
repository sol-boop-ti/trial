# Reference Teaser

A reference video and a brand go in; a teaser of about twenty seconds with the reference's cuts, rhythm and devices and the brand's story comes out. The reference is the taste, the brand is the content, the human is touched once.

## Mental Model

The reference is decomposed once into a beat list measured off frames and audio, and every later step transcribes that list instead of interpreting the video. The brand arrives as two documents written without asking anyone: a brand kit and a story brief. Everything up to the gate is the work of the one agent that will ask; the team is created from the answer. The one gate shows the swap copy, the storyboard and the renderer routing side by side, so a wrong story, a wrong look or a wrong renderer is an edit before the build instead of a re-render after it.

## Key Rules

1. **Beat list first; nothing is built until it exists.** The reference arrives as a link; the video itself is fetched and read frame by frame. A link that yields no watchable video stops the run with a report of what the link returned, because every row below is measured from its frames and a list written from a description or a transcript is not a beat list. One row per reference cut, measured from frames and audio: in/out frame, hold, text verbatim, camera and kinetic move, transition out, the hit it lands on. Plus a drop map from onsets with the tempo they sit on, rhythm notes (cut rate, energy shape, who leads), a motion grammar (every recurring device named as a preset, with the rows it occurs on and its timing spec, so a device is built once and reused on every row that carries it), and a register lock read off the same frames and audio: grade and light, texture, type weight and case, motion feel, sound mix and voice. The register lock constrains every later step; it is not a note. A reference decomposed before is loaded, not redone.
2. **Twenty seconds is the aim, not the cap.** A row whose copy or UI needs more time than its reference hold gets it, and the film runs long rather than rushing a row; no hold grows past what its content needs, so nothing drags. The list stays at reference length; the `retimed` view takes the reference's densest contiguous twenty-second stretch, two stretches at most joined at one of its own cuts, and keeps every row inside it, inserts and reaction rows included, so the teaser carries the reference's cut-rate shape: its open, its burst, its exhale. Holds are never scaled and a row is never dropped as filler: a sixty-second reference with sixteen cuts yields a teaser with eight to eleven cuts, not five.
3. **Components are researched before the kit is written.** Every retimed row that puts UI in frame names what it needs to look finished: a real chart, a dashboard, a command palette, a cursor, a device frame, a metric ticking up. Each named piece is sourced, in this order: the company's public repository when it is open source, so the film shows the product's own components; then the stock library's components; then the `21st-dev` connector for anything neither has, searched by what the row needs, not by the company's name. Along with the component comes how it animates, read from its own code where the source carries motion, so the row's entrance, life and exit use the component's own movement rather than a slide laid on top. Nothing is built from scratch that one of the three already has, and never screenshots. The rows with their sourced component and its move are the kit's product UI, so the kit is written knowing what it must carry.
4. **Kit and story brief are written autonomously, once per company.** Kit from the public repository when open source, else the live site (`brand-kit-authoring`). The kit holds identity only: logo, primary colour, font family, product UI, mascot, and a voice name that applies to voice-over only, never to a speaker in the Seedance take. It carries no law on temperature, texture, motion feel or sound; a film's look is composed after its beat list exists, from the kit's identity and the reference's register lock, and where the two meet the reference wins. Found components are restyled on the kit's tokens. Brief fields: what they sell, who watches, the one claim, the reveal, proof with a source per figure, at most three beats, closing line and URL, voice and register, words required and banned.
5. **The swap table is derived from the brief.** Same shot numbers and cut frames; only text and identity tokens change, with a one-line reason on every departing line; temperature, texture, motion feel and sound register stay the reference's. Figures come only from the proof field.
6. **The renderer follows the world.** Text, UI filling the frame, brand assets and sub-second hits are Remotion. Anything physical, a person, a place, a material, a camera through space, is Seedance. The reference sets who leads; a routing column marks every row, with one line naming what earns each Seedance row.
7. **Keyframes are the storyboard.** One first frame per shot, plus an end-state where a shot resolves, rendered from the composition with real kit files. A Remotion row's keyframe is that render and nothing else. A Seedance row's keyframe is a composited image: the composition renders the row's product UI flat from the kit, and `openai/gpt-image-2` places that render into the row's world, a plate with a character reference when the row has one. The UI inside the composite is the composition's render, never redrawn; readable copy still lives in the Remotion layer on top.
8. **One gate, before the team exists.** The supervisor does everything above itself: decomposition, kit, brief, swap table, routing, keyframes. It then asks once, with one question fragment of three sections in one card: the brief with the swap table (is the script right for this brand), the look section (is this the same film for this brand): every keyframe paired at the same size with the reference frame it recreates, the register lock with the kit's answer beside each line, and a three-second render of one row, since stills carry neither motion nor sound; the routing column (is the routing right). Each section has an approve control and a free-text edit field; one explicit confirm returns all three at once. Duration, ratio, register and structure are fixed by the reference and the brief, so none of them is a question. On the answer the team is created with the brief, the table, the routing, the keyframes and every edit written into the project manager's task; no one on the team asks the user anything. Nothing else waits on the user.
9. **Then straight through.** Timeline from the beat list, one Seedance take under 25 references and 25 seconds with two seeds, slices trimmed so the strongest moment hits the drop frame, the bed laid under the picture whole, QA on frames and on the beat. Garbled spans are covered or rebuilt in Remotion, never re-rolled. When anyone in the Seedance take speaks, the take is generated with audio and that audio is the speech track; it is never muted, replaced or re-voiced. Presets fold back into the kit.
10. **Music is one take; the cut snaps to it, never the reverse.** The bed is generated at the tempo the beat list measured off the reference and plays as one continuous piece from its first downbeat at frame 0 to the last frame. It is never cut, re-anchored, looped or stretched to land a drop: a listener holds the pulse, and a seam that moves it by less than a beat is heard as a stumble even when every onset sits on a grid. A drop that misses the bed's beat is landed by moving the cut frame to the bed's nearest eighth, or by an accent laid on top of the bed when the cut cannot move; the bed itself does not change. The check runs on the rendered master: every beat one beat from the last, first frame to last.
11. **Voice is one generation.** Speech from anyone in the Seedance take comes from that take. When a UGC actor is heard but not shown, the whole teaser is still one Seedance take with audio on; Remotion covers the picture and the take's audio stays. Text-to-speech is only for voice-over, when nobody in the take speaks: then the whole voice-over is a single `google/gemini-3.1-flash-tts` call, with `[short pause]`, `[medium pause]` and `[long pause]` tags placed where the beat list breathes, so the read is one continuous take whose pauses land the cuts (`gemini-3-1-flash-tts`).
12. **Something survives every cut.** Every row has an entrance, a life and an exit, and at least one thing crosses each cut: an element that persists, a camera move that continues, or an exit that overlaps the next entrance. No frame holds still for more than a second. Rows may premount and exits may run past the row boundary; the cut frame is where the picture changes, not where motion stops. A reference row whose camera is locked still enters and exits, in the reference's motion feel.
13. **Every generated image is `openai/gpt-image-2`.** Plates, character references, the composited Seedance keyframes and any other still the film needs come from that one model, so every image in a run shares one look and one prompt grammar, and the Seedance take is referenced from images of the same family. Remotion rows never touch the image model.
