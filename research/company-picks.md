# Prospect Video Picks (D2)

Source: `data/series-b-bay-area.csv` (38 companies). Date of analysis: 2026-09-24. Higgsfield excluded per brief.

## Scoring criteria
1. **Freshness of round.** A company that just raised has fresh budget, a hiring and launch push, and a funding announcement to amplify. This is the moment it needs video.
2. **Named marketing or creative buyer** in the CSV (someone to actually send the video to).
3. **Visual product.** The UI or experience makes rich video material, which suits the reference-teaser skill.
4. **Fit with video as a need.** Would their marketing team plausibly buy video production?

## Picks

### 1. Flam: freshest round with a real buyer
- **Series B of $40M announced 10 days ago** (2026-09-14). The team is in announce, hire and launch mode right now.
- The CSV lists both the **CMO and the Creative Director**, the exact people who buy video.
- They sell interactive content to enterprise marketers, so they judge visual quality professionally. An impressive video lands, and a mediocre one doesn't. That makes it a strong showcase.
- Watch-out: the HQ flag (SF per the company, Bengaluru roots). It doesn't matter for a video pitch.

### 2. Wispr Flow: biggest fresh round, design-led marketing
- **$280M Series B, 38 days ago**. The largest round on the list after Higgsfield.
- Contacts are the **VP Product Marketing and Head of Design, Marketing**. It's a design-led brand that ships polished launch content.
- They're **moving into meetings**, a product expansion that will need launch videos soon. That gives the email a natural hook: "here's what your meetings launch could look like".
- The product is very visual and easy to understand (speak → text appears everywhere), so it's ideal for a 20s teaser with kinetic type and UI.
- Segment flag (prosumer + business) is acceptable: they have a B2B motion and a big marketing budget.

### Backup: Convex
If either pick yields a weak brand kit: Convex is **open source**, and the reference-teaser skill (rule 3) pulls UI components from the company's public repo, so the film would show the product's *real* components. That's a strong technical story.
